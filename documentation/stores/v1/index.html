03

Epic Games Store: open it

  1. Create the organisation on the Epic Developer Portal as Todak Studios Sdn Bhd, using the Studios shared mailbox as the owner and the same Epic account the studio already uses for the Unreal Engine and Fab. Accept the Epic Online Services terms; verify the address.
  2. Turn on the store publishing tools for the organisation, then create the first product. Identity and payout details follow, as on Steam.
  3. Pay USD 100 per game when submitting. It comes back once the game earns.
  4. What it adds: a second PC storefront that takes nothing on the first million per game per year, and a proper home for the studio's Unreal relationship, since the regional Epic team is already writing to the studio.
  5. For the kit: one more publishing target beside Steam; the same build, a different uploader. Add it after Steam is wired, not before.
04

Roblox: a lane, not a rung

Roblox is a platform with its own editor, its own language and its own money. A Godot or Unity game cannot be uploaded to it. So the question is not "add Roblox to the list" but "do we want a Roblox lane at all". Two things say yes, and 2026's rules say do it properly.

  • The money is knocking. RIVRS, a Roblox publisher, opened a USD 1 million creator fund for Southeast Asia and MDEC sent it to Neo in March. It offers financing, publishing and marketing support to studios entering Roblox, and it acquires promising games. That is a paid way to learn a new platform.
  • Lan already has something. Since July, people outside the company have been asking Lan for access to a "Roblox Studio" item in his Drive. Whatever it is, a course, a template, a project, it is a thread to pull before starting from zero.
  • 2026 rules. Publishing for all ages now needs an ID-verified, age-checked account with two-step verification in good standing, plus either a Roblox Plus subscription or a 1,000 Robux fee per game to reach players under 16 (since 19 May). Creator Store uploads need an age-checked account (since 26 August). Cash-out needs 30,000 earned Robux. A studio publishes through a group owned by a verified company account, so that account has to be a real person's identity on the company's behalf.
  • Recommendation. Open the studio account and group now, under the Studios shared identity with a named admin, and apply to RIVRS. Run one small Roblox experience as part of the Unity-and-engines exploration in Unity lane v1, with the Studios crew, not with cohort 1. If it works, Roblox becomes an elective for cohort 2.
05

Housekeeping before students touch these accounts

  • Steam banking details. Steam warned in September 2025 that the studio's banking information was about to expire. Payments have continued through August 2026, so it was probably renewed, but confirm it in the partner site before a cohort's capstone week.
  • ToGather: Island. Still labelled Early Access, last developer update over five years ago, with a "full release end of 2021" promise on the page. Either ship a small update through the new pipeline, which is a good first job for the Unity lane, or change the page's wording. A stale Early Access page is what a store reviewer sees first when the studio submits a new game.
  • Who holds the keys. itch.io and Steamworks both sit on the shared studio mailbox, with sign-ins from several staff over the years. Move both into the Studios password manager with two-factor on the mailbox, and list the named admins. The kit's publisher desk needs a service login on each, scoped to uploads only.
  • Personal accounts. Three staff have personal itch.io accounts and several have personal Epic and Steam accounts. Fine for players; nothing of the studio's should be published from them.
  • Package and naming rules from Blueprint v2 apply to Steam and Epic app names too, because a store name is hard to change after launch.

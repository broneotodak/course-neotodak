What the student sees, how we teach, what it runs on

Neo's question of 20 September: "If I am a student and I walk in, what should I expect? A Claude Code terminal? Codex? A front end for both, where I type in one place and see the result in another?" This page answers it from Claude Code's side and from Astra's side, then prices the seats, the machines and the services around it. The profit split decided the same day (50% Todak Studios, 20% Todak Academy, 30% the student) is recorded in Blueprint v2.1.

Short version
  1. The screenOne workbench, two desks, one game window. The student works in an editor workspace the kit sets up: a Design desk (Astra) and a Build desk (Claude Code) as side panels, one open at a time until week 7, the code in the middle, and the game itself running in the Godot window next to it. No raw terminal on day 1. The terminal is a skill taught in week 4.
  2. Build or buyThe two tools' own editor panels already give "type here, see it there" once the Godot game window is the "there". We build the thin layer around them: the Todak sidebar with today's steps, the design board, the build and publish buttons and the prompt journey. Weeks. A browser-based "Todak Studio" that hides both tools is phase 2, only if cohort 1 shows the need. Months.
  3. A lessonThree hours: a 15-minute demo on the projector, a design warm-up with Astra, a guided build with Claude Code and Godot, an AI-off walkthrough, a showcase, and five minutes of saving to memory. Section 04.
  4. Astra's viewAstra was asked the same question, in writing, and answered on its own. It agrees on a prepared workspace beside Godot and a small Todak panel, prefers one AI panel open at a time, and flags two gaps in v2. Section 06 has its words and where we differ.
  5. SeatsOne Claude seat and one Astra seat per student, paid for in the fee, as Neo decided. Buy them as Team and Business seats the Academy administers: same price as the consumer plans, and the seat is handed to the next student by an admin instead of rotating a password.
  6. MachinesWhat Todak already owns covers almost everything. Buy one Mac mini (M6, 32 GB, 1 TB, in stores 22 Sep). The course database, the collector and the showcase run on what exists.
  7. Local AIYes, for images in bulk, 3D and sound, on the idle bandung-01 box first, through the kit. Ideas, moodboards, storyboards, character concepts and presentations are already covered by the two seats.

▶ The game, runningPress Play, or say "run it" to either desk.
  • The "type here, see it there" Neo described is real in this layout. The student types in a desk, the code changes in the middle, Godot reloads it and the game runs on the right. No custom viewer is needed for that; Godot is the viewer.
  • One monitor? The layout still works: the game window floats over the desks while it runs. Two 24-inch monitors per lab PC make the day noticeably calmer and cost little. Decision 6.
  • Under the hood the Workbench is a VS Code profile installed by the kit, with the official Claude Code and Codex editor panels, the Godot tools, and one small Todak extension that draws the sidebar and calls the kit's commands. Nothing exotic.
  • The lecturer's projector shows the lecturer's own Workbench during demos, and the class dashboard from the classroom system during work time: who is on which step, whose build just went live, the showcase links.

Why not the terminal first. A black window with a blinking cursor tells a beginner nothing about where they are or what to do next. The desks in the Workbench are chat panels with the code beside them. The terminal arrives in week 4 as a skill, with the no-AI debugging drills, when the student already knows what the commands are for.

06

Astra's point of view

Astra was given a written brief (the fixed facts from v2 and Neo's question) and asked for its own answer, in its own words, with no draft from Claude Code to copy. It could read the site and search the web. What follows is its answer, lightly trimmed, then where the two views differ and what we take from it.

The screen. "Choose the middle option: a prepared workspace with a small Todak lesson panel, beside Godot. The student's first experience should be playing something, changing it and explaining the difference. Using two untouched apps is quickest, but leaves beginners managing windows and handovers. A complete Todak Studio could look simpler, but makes us responsible for a software product before we have tested the teaching." Astra uses the same editor and the same two official panels we do, and adds one rule: "Open one AI panel at a time." A terminal "opens only for a taught task".

Day 1. "Students sign in, see their name and today's task, and play a supplied practice scene. They change one visible setting themselves. Claude Code then guides one recorded task and saves its outcome to project memory. Astra stays closed until the lecturer introduces the design desk."

The design desk. "Show the current design beside two or three alternatives. The student chooses and explains why before we build." Moodboard, character sheet, storyboard, interface mock-up and the one-page design document are pictures and text the student keeps, in the repository: "Chat alone is not the deliverable."

Handing over. "After playtesting, the student hands Claude Code the branch and a short note: intention, changed files, tests, known problems and asset sources. Stop Astra's work first. Claude Code checks it, returns specific fixes or merges approved changes into the main project and updates memory. Neither tool silently overwrites the other's work."

The projector. "Show one decision, its changed file and its result. Keep individual prompt histories and assessment notes off the projector."

Build or buy. "Two weeks for a demonstrator, then six to ten further weeks for a dependable pilot," assuming two experienced developers and a lecturer testing weekly. A full custom Studio is "three to six months or more, plus maintenance." And: "I disagree with v2 section 11's two-week kit scope covering every genre and all services."

What v2 still needs to confront. (1) "Experienced staff shipping reference games proves the pipeline. Add observed trials with four to six actual beginners before enrolment." (2) Test the recording end to end, "long prompts, restarts, offline queues and both interfaces. Show saved versus waiting to upload; a recording fault needs recovery, not an automatic student penalty." (3) On shared PCs, "verify work and records reached storage before logout." (4) "AI scores need lecturer moderation. A publish approval or Neo bypass does not prove competence. Simultaneous class builds need a queue."

The full text is at astra-pov.md, with Astra's own lesson plan for a week-3 session.

07

Accounts and seats

Decided by Neo: each student gets their own account for the term, on an Academy-owned email, the password rotated at graduation, the cost absorbed in the fee. Below is the cleanest way to run exactly that.

  • Buy the seats as a Claude Team workspace and a ChatGPT Business workspace owned by the Academy. A standard Claude Team seat is about USD 20 a month billed yearly or USD 25 monthly, includes Claude Code, and the plan starts at two seats. A ChatGPT Business seat is about USD 20 a month billed yearly or USD 25 monthly, with Codex included at the same allowance as a Plus account. Both prices are as published in September 2026 and worth re-checking at purchase.
  • Same money as the consumer plans, fewer chores. At enrolment an admin adds the student to a seat. At graduation the admin removes them and the seat goes to the next student. No password rotation, no shared logins (which the consumer terms do not allow anyway), one invoice, and the workspace's content is not used for training by default.
  • One person per seat at a time. The Academy owns the workspace, the student brings nothing and takes nothing but their published games and their journey export.
  • Heavy weeks. The Claude allowance will be hit in the 3D weeks; Blueprint v2 already keeps a buffer of five Claude Max seats for the capstone month. Plan the same buffer on the Astra side, a few higher-tier seats or extra credits for weeks 7–11, and confirm the amount in the dry run.
  • What the seats already cover. Ideas, moodboards, storyboards, character concepts, design documents, presentations and slide decks: Astra makes pictures inside its seat (the RUSH icon and cover were made this way) and Claude makes documents and slides inside its seat. No extra tool is needed for any of that.

Which Mac mini to buy

The 2026 lineup reaches stores on 22 September. Two chips: M6 from RM3,799 (education RM3,399) with 16 GB and 256 GB, and M5 Pro from RM7,499 (education RM7,099) with 24 GB and 512 GB. The build farm is a processor-and-disk job, not a graphics job, so the M6 is enough. What matters is memory and disk.

ConfigurationPriceVerdict
M6 · 16 GB · 256 GBRM3,799 (edu RM3,399)No. Xcode, the iPhone simulators, the Android tools and Godot's templates alone fill the disk, and 16 GB chokes when an iPhone build and an Android build run together.
M6 · 32 GB · 1 TBStock configuration on apple.com/my; price to confirm at the store, expect roughly RM6kRecommended. Runs an Xcode build, a Godot export and the headless reviewer at the same time, with room for twenty students' build caches. Serves every cohort.
M5 Pro · 24 GB · 512 GBRM7,499 (edu RM7,099)Only if Neo wants headroom to run local models on the farm later. More chip than the farm needs, less disk.
  • Standard 2.5 Gb Ethernet is fine; the office network and the NAS do not need the 10 Gb option.
  • Education pricing applies if the Academy qualifies as an institution on Apple's education store. Worth a call before ordering.
  • Setup is a copy of the recipe that built RUSH and ApaNakMakan: Xcode with the Studios signing certificates in a dedicated keychain, the Android tools with the upload key from the vault, Godot with export templates, and the fleet's own hands agent so builds can be driven from the kit.

Local AI for students: yes, in this order

  1. The seats first. Text, ideas, design documents, moodboards, storyboards, character concepts, presentations: the two seats do all of this already, and the cost is in the fee.
  2. The fleet for pictures in bulk, 3D and sound. Sprites, tilesets and textures by the dozen run on the Academy AI box (bandung-01, with tr-home as overflow) through the kit's asset service, with a queue and a per-student meter. 3D models go through Todak's own photo-to-3D pipeline on EdgeXpert, outside class hours or queued. Music and sound effects through the fleet's existing song and sound lanes or free libraries with store-safe licences.
  3. Paid services only for what the fleet cannot do well, through the kit with Todak's own accounts and a per-student budget of about RM50 (the v1 figure stands). A pay-per-use provider for higher-quality 3D when the pipeline falls short; the Studios crew's video tools for showcase trailers. Retail subscriptions per student (Meshy and the like) are dropped.

Two licence flags. The twin's voice engine on EdgeXpert is licensed for personal use only, so student games must not ship voices from it; use a properly licensed voice service on a Todak account, metered, or a voice tool with a commercial licence, to verify. And Higgsfield is Neo's personal subscription, driven through its own login; it is not a tool twenty students share.

This session's presentation

First lab · 60 minutes

Play, then ask.

  • Objective: every student gets their paddle moving and explains the change in one sentence.
  • The game is already built. Nobody installs anything today.
  • Two helpers behind one box: Astra for pictures, Claude Code for how the game behaves.
0–5 min

Play it first.

  • Click inside the game, press Space. The computer plays the right paddle.
  • Ask the room: what is wrong with this game? (Two things: your paddle, and the ball flies through.)
  • That gap is today's lesson.
5–20 min

One change, in your own words.

  • "My paddle doesn't move." Watch the badge: Build desk · Claude Code, and the reason.
  • The game rebuilds itself. Play again.
  • Then the desk asks what changed. One sentence, no jargon, unlocks the next step.

Walk the room. The board on the right shows who has the tick and who is stuck.

20–35 min

The other helper.

  • "Draw me a cool title screen." Badge: Design desk · Astra. A picture lands on the design board.
  • Ask: why did the box choose differently? Let students guess the route before sending.
  • Optional: "make the ball faster" — back to the build desk.
35–45 min

Try to cheat.

  • "Build me the whole game." The box answers with the step list and "not in one go".
  • Say it out loud: the course is steps, and the box will not let anyone skip.
45–60 min

Read the code. See the record.

  • Open the Code tab together: the paddle diff, line by line, in plain words.
  • Open the Journey tab: everything they typed, with the route and the reason.
  • Close: in week 4 the AI goes off for the drills. Today they learned the box; the code is theirs.
